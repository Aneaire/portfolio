## Section 2: Project Management & Delivery

### "Describe your project management process."

> I work in 2-week sprints. At the start, I scope the work with the client and break it into tasks with clear acceptance criteria. During the sprint, I track progress daily — checking blockers, updating statuses, and keeping communication flowing. At the end, we review deliverables with the client and run a quick retro to improve the next cycle. The tools I use depend on the team, but I'm comfortable with Notion, Linear, GitHub, and Slack.

### "How do you handle scope creep?"

> I address it directly but diplomatically. When a client adds something mid-sprint, I don't just say no — I show the trade-off. "We can add this, but it means X gets pushed to next sprint. Are you okay with that?" Having a clear backlog and sprint commitment makes this conversation easy because it's not about saying no — it's about making the cost visible so the client can make an informed decision.

### "How do you ensure quality in deliverables?"

> Three layers. First, clear acceptance criteria upfront so everyone knows what "done" looks like. Second, I make sure deliverables are testable before they go to the client — not just "it works" but "it works as specified." Third, I build in review steps. Whether that's a QA pass, a peer review, or a client UAT session, there's always a checkpoint before something ships.

### "Tell me about a project that didn't go well. What did you learn?"

> *(Personalize this — here's a framework:)*
>
> In one project, I underestimated how long client feedback cycles would take. I planned the sprint assuming quick turnaround on approvals, but the client had internal review processes I didn't account for. The sprint slipped by several days.
>
> What I learned: always map out the client's side of the process too, not just your own. Now I explicitly ask about internal approval workflows during kickoff and build buffer time for client-side delays.

## Section 3: Client Communication

### "How do you handle a difficult or unhappy client?"

> First, I listen without being defensive. Usually, frustration comes from unmet expectations — and that's often a communication gap, not a work quality issue. I acknowledge their concern, clarify what happened, and present a concrete plan to resolve it. Then I follow through quickly. The key is being proactive — if I see something going sideways, I flag it before the client has to ask. Surprises erode trust faster than delays do.

### "How do you run client meetings effectively?"

> Every meeting has an agenda shared in advance, even if it's three bullet points. During the call, I capture decisions and action items in real-time. After the call, I send a summary with owners and deadlines within the hour. This creates accountability and ensures nothing gets lost in conversation. I also time-box discussions — if something needs a deeper dive, I schedule a focused follow-up rather than derailing the current meeting.

### "How do you handle a client who keeps changing requirements?"

> I differentiate between natural evolution and true scope creep. If requirements are shifting because the client is learning what they need (which is normal in AI projects), I adapt the approach — maybe shorter cycles, more prototyping, earlier demos. If it's scope creep, I make the trade-off visible: "Adding this means we deprioritize Y. Which matters more to you right now?" The goal is always to keep the client informed and in control of decisions, not surprised by consequences.

## Quick Tips for the Interview

1. **Be specific** — use real examples, not hypotheticals
2. **Show curiosity about AI** — ask questions about their tech stack, what AI projects look like, what tools the team uses
3. **Demonstrate ownership** — they want someone proactive, not someone who waits to be told
4. **Keep answers under 2 minutes** — structured, concise, then stop
5. **Ask smart questions** at the end (see below)

## Questions to Ask Them

- "What does a typical 2-week sprint look like for the AI team?"
- "What are the biggest challenges your PMs face with client delivery right now?"
- "What tools does the team currently use for project tracking and communication?"
- "How does the team handle knowledge sharing about AI/automation best practices?"
- "What does success look like in this role in the first 90 days?"
- "How many client projects would I be managing initially?"
