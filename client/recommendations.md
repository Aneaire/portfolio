# Recommendations for the proposal

## Recommended scope and timeline

Do not promise one month for the complete scope. A one-month build is possible for a small MVP with a fixed design and a simple CMS. The safer estimate for the requested version is 5–7 weeks.

Suggested phases:

1. Discovery and visual direction: 3–5 days
2. Data model, CMS and post builder: 1.5–2 weeks
3. Website experience and responsive layouts: 1.5–2 weeks
4. Likes, search, filtering, analytics and deployment: 1 week
5. Content entry, testing and revisions: 3–5 days

## Suggested pricing

Use the range that matches your experience and the amount of custom work you expect:

- Lean MVP: ₱100,000–₱140,000
- Full initial version: ₱150,000–₱220,000
- More advanced visual editor or video workflow: quote separately after discovery

Keep hosting, domain registration, media storage, analytics services and other third-party costs separate from your development fee.

## Revision and support policy

Use language like this in the proposal:

> Two rounds of revisions are included during development. Revisions cover changes to the agreed design and functionality. New features, major changes to the approved direction or additional integrations will be estimated separately.

> One week of post-launch support is included for bugs and minor adjustments related to the delivered scope. New features, major design changes and ongoing content entry are billed separately.

## Payment structure

A practical structure is:

- 40% deposit before work begins
- 30% after the CMS and first working website version are ready
- 30% after final revisions and handover

Do not transfer ownership of the source repository or deployment accounts until the final payment is complete. The client should still own the accounts from the beginning, while you are added as a collaborator.

## Important scope questions for the meeting

- How should a post behave when it contains both portrait and landscape media?
- Should videos autoplay, loop or require a tap?
- Are likes anonymous, or should visitors create accounts?
- Should the archive be public, private or partially private?
- What does the client mean by returning visitors and general location in analytics?
- How much media is expected, and who pays for storage and bandwidth?
- Is the CMS only for the client, or will multiple people manage it?
- Does the client already have a preferred domain, hosting provider or analytics tool?

The most important question is whether likes require accounts. Anonymous likes are simpler. Accounts, profiles and protection against repeated likes would add meaningful work.
