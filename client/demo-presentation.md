# Personal visual archive demo

Working prototype: `/client-demo`

## Purpose

This is a recording-ready presentation for showing the client how the website and CMS could work. It is a product concept, not a claim that the full application has already been completed.

Aim for a 4–6 minute recording. Keep the demo visual and explain the decisions in plain language.

## Slide 1: Opening

**On screen:** Open the working prototype on its latest entry. Keep the browser at an iPad-sized viewport if your recorder supports it.

**Say:**

> I put together a working concept based on your brief. It shows how the website could feel, how visitors could browse the archive and how you could manage posts without needing a developer for everyday updates.

## Slide 2: The main archive

**On screen:** Show the main entry and the preview of the next date. Click the right-hand preview to move forward.

**Say:**

> The homepage puts the posts first. On a larger tablet, two entries can sit next to each other. On mobile, the same experience becomes a focused single-post view so the media remains easy to see.

## Slide 3: Horizontal browsing

**On screen:** Use the arrow controls or click the next post preview. Change between two or three entries.

**Say:**

> The main archive can be browsed chronologically. Visitors can move horizontally from recent entries to older ones, which makes the site feel more like a personal visual timeline than a traditional blog.

## Slide 4: A post with mixed media

**On screen:** Click the small media thumbnails below the main image. Point out that each frame keeps its own caption.

**Say:**

> Each post can contain a different number of photos or videos. Portrait and landscape media can live in the same entry, and every item can have its own caption. The layout can adapt to the content instead of forcing every image into one fixed shape.

## Slide 5: Archive and search

**On screen:** Click “Archive,” then choose an older entry. Open search and type “sea” or “home.”

**Say:**

> The archive view gives visitors a quicker way to understand the collection and jump to a specific period. Search and filters can be added without taking attention away from the main visual experience.

## Slide 6: Likes and analytics

**On screen:** Like the current post. Click “Open studio” and pause briefly on the dashboard metrics.

**Say:**

> Likes can be included without adding comments. The analytics view can show useful high-level information such as visits, returning visitors, general location, traffic sources and which posts receive the most attention. The exact setup will depend on the privacy and analytics tool we choose.

## Slide 7: Creating a post in the CMS

**On screen:** Click “New post.” Edit the title, select different media items and update the caption in the right-hand inspector.

**Say:**

> This is the part I would design most carefully with you. To create a post, you would open the admin panel, add the date and title, upload your photos or videos, arrange their order, write captions and save the entry as a draft or publish it.

## Slide 8: Editing individual media

**On screen:** Select each media item and show its independent caption. The prototype displays drag handles to communicate the intended arranging workflow.

**Say:**

> Each item can be managed independently. You can update a caption, change the order or remove one item without rebuilding the entire post. This is why the CMS workflow needs to be agreed before development begins.

## Slide 9: Ownership and handover

**On screen:** Stay in the CMS and explain ownership over the code, accounts and data. This prototype does not include a real backend yet.

**Say:**

> The domain, hosting, database, source code and CMS account would be under your ownership. I would help set everything up and provide documentation so another developer can take over if needed.

## Slide 10: Proposed next step

**On screen:** A short list: confirm visual direction, define post builder, approve scope, begin development.

**Say:**

> The next step would be to agree on the visual direction and define exactly how you want to create a post. Once that is approved, I can provide the final quotation, milestones and delivery date. My current estimate for the initial version is approximately five to seven weeks.

## Recording notes

- Use sample content that looks like a personal archive, but do not use the client’s private photos without permission.
- Show the same post on desktop, iPad and mobile if possible.
- Demonstrate one complete flow from creating a CMS draft to viewing the published post.
- Keep the prototype focused. A polished flow with three sample posts is more convincing than many unfinished screens.
- Call it a “working concept” or “interface prototype.” Do not describe it as previous client work or a finished production system.
- End by asking which parts of the post builder matter most to her. That turns the recording into the start of the discovery conversation.
